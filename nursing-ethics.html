Unit III: Ethical Principles & Theories

1. Ethical Theories in Healthcare

Deontology (Kantian Ethics)

Proposed by Immanuel Kant. Asserts that actions are intrinsically right or wrong based on moral duties, regardless of outcomes.

Utilitarianism (Consequentialism)

Proposed by Bentham & Mill. Focuses on outcomes: an action is right if it produces the "greatest good for the greatest number of people".

2. The Four Core Principles (Beauchamp & Childress)

Ethical Principle Clinical Definition Nursing Application Example
Autonomy Respecting a patient's right to self-determination. Obtaining informed consent; respecting treatment refusal.
Non-Maleficence Do no intentional or avoidable harm. Double-checking high-alert medication doses.
Beneficence Actions performed for the benefit and well-being of others. Providing pain relief and repositioning immobile clients.
Justice Fairness in resource distribution and non-discriminatory care. Emergency department triage based on medical urgency.

Unit IV: Confidentiality & Informed Consent

1. Definitions & Clinical Importance

Confidentiality: The ethical and legal duty to protect private client information from unauthorized disclosure.

Informed Consent: The voluntary, legal agreement given by a competent patient to undergo a specific medical procedure.

📌 Crucial Exam Point: The physician explains the procedure and obtains consent. The nurse acts as a witness to the signature, verifying voluntariness and patient capacity.
